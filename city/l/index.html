Three churches

Into Poperinge by Werf, out by Kleine Casseldreef; the old town in between.

9,3 kmabout 2 h 05Poperinge

Good to know

Lanes to town, pavement in it. Shops close on Sunday; the churches are usually open in the daytime, the Weeuwhof gate too. Market on Friday mornings on Grote Markt.

Poperinge got its town rights in 1147 and grew rich on cloth, then on hops, which is why the Hopmuseum sits in the old weighing house and why there are three Gothic churches for a town this size.

More

The three churches are the mark of the medieval cloth town: Sint-Bertinus for the centre, Sint-Jan and Onze-Lieve-Vrouw for the parishes that grew around it. Hop growing took over from the 15th century and Poperinge is still the hop capital of Belgium, with the Hoppefeesten every three years in September.

Out of the door

Down the gravel lane to Kleine Dasdreef, then left, up the dreef to Helleketelweg and right, towards town.

1,1 km

Past Nine Elms

A small war cemetery between the fields on Helleketelweg, ten minutes from the door. Worth the two-minute detour through the gate even on a town walk.

Most people in Poperinge do not know it is here.

Gasthuisstraat

Werf and Duinkerkestraat bring you into the main shopping street. The Hopmuseum is in the old municipal weighing house where the hop bales were weighed and stamped; Talbot House is a few doors on.

The Gasthuiskapel

At the end of the street, the chapel of the medieval hospital, now an exhibition space; look in if the door is open.

3,5 km

Grote Markt and Sint-Bertinus

The square with the neo-Gothic town hall of 1911 and, behind it, Sint-Bertinuskerk, the main church, with its heavy tower. Cafés all round the square for a coffee.

Halfway. Sit down.

South and west through the back streets

5,5 km

Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk

The oldest of the three, 13th century, a hall church in a green square. The parish outgrew it; it stands quiet now.

The Weeuwhof

Through the gate on Sint-Annastraat: a courtyard of small 18th-century almshouses built for widows, still lived in, with benches under the trees.

The quietest spot in town.
