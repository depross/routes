Behind the front

The town behind the Salient, and the biggest hospital cemetery of the war.

16,1 kmabout 3 h 40Poperinge

Good to know

Pavement in town, lanes and a cycle path outside it. Everything is open around the clock except Talbot House (from 10:00), the death cells (closed Mondays) and the Lijssenthoek visitor centre (9:00 to 18:00). Allow extra time: this is a day with four stops worth lingering at.

Poperinge was never taken in the First World War. It sat eleven kilometres behind Ypres, out of range of most guns, and became the place where the soldiers of the Salient slept, drank, were patched up and, for a few, were shot.

More

Nine Elms was the cemetery of two casualty clearing stations, set up in the fields west of the town in September 1917 for the Passchendaele offensive. Talbot House took its name from Gilbert Talbot, killed at Hooge in 1915; his brother Neville founded the club with Philip Clayton, and the Toc H movement grew out of it after the war. The death cells and the execution post behind the town hall were restored and opened as a memorial; the New Military Cemetery holds seventeen of the men who died there.

The walk

0,0 km

Out of the door

Down the gravel lane to Kleine Dasdreef, then left, up the dreef to Helleketelweg and right, towards town.

1,1 km

Nine Elms British Cemetery

Ten minutes from the door, between the fields on Helleketelweg: 1,556 graves from the casualty clearing stations that stood here in 1917 and 1918. Among them Dave Gallaher, captain of the 1905 All Blacks, killed at Passchendaele.

Most people in Poperinge do not know it is here.

3,1 km

Into town by Werf and Gasthuisstraat

Werf and Duinkerkestraat bring you into Gasthuisstraat, the street where the soldiers' Poperinge happened: cafés, shops, the Hopmuseum in the old weighing house, and halfway along, number 43.

3,2 km

Talbot House

The Everyman's Club opened by chaplains Tubby Clayton and Neville Talbot in December 1915, where rank was left at the door and the chapel was in the hop loft. Still a house, still open, still serving tea in the garden.

Open daily 10:00 to 17:30. Give it an hour if you go in.

3,6 km

The death cells and the execution post

In the courtyard behind the town hall on Grote Markt: the cells where condemned soldiers spent their last night, and the post against which they were shot at dawn. Free, quiet, and the hardest minutes of the walk.

Closed Mondays.

4,8 km

Poperinghe New Military Cemetery

South-east of the centre on Deken De Bolaan. 677 Commonwealth and 270 French burials, and seventeen soldiers executed in the town; more than in any other cemetery of the war.

8,5 km

South on Boescheepseweg

Deken De Bolaan becomes Boescheepseweg and leaves town between the fields. Three kilometres later, on the right, the visitor centre of Lijssenthoek (free, open 9:00 to 18:00).

8,7 km

Lijssenthoek Military Cemetery

The hospital cemetery of the Salient: 10,784 graves beside the railway siding where the casualty clearing stations stood. Almost everyone here died in a hospital bed, not in battle. One woman among them, nurse Nellie Spindler.

The second largest Commonwealth cemetery in Belgium.
