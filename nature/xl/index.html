Three woods

North to the woods and the castle park, home along Provenseweg.

17,3 kmabout 3 h 55Poperinge

Good to know

A long one on quiet lanes, with three woods and the old railway as the unpaved parts. Two roads to respect: Couthoflaan north of Couthofbos and the ring road at Poperinge; both have a crossing within a few metres of the line.

Nothing to eat or drink on the way except what you carry; the one café is De Lovie's on weekdays. Plan a good half day.

More

Couthofbos and the Couthof estate are the oldest woodland around Poperinge. Canadabos is named after the Canadian troops of 1944 and still hides a V1 launch base in the undergrowth. De Lovie, between them, is a neo-Gothic castle of the 1850s in a landscaped park with ponds, now a care institution that opens its gardens to visitors.

The way out follows the old railway to Sint-Jan-ter-Biezen; the way back follows Provenseweg, which was the medieval road from Poperinge to Proven.

The walk

Clockwise. Distances are from the front door.

0,0 km

Out of the door

Down the gravel lane to Kleine Dasdreef, then left and west on Helleketelweg; at the Karel Grimminckweg junction take Duivinnestraat north.

Clockwise. The woods come between kilometre six and eleven.

2,8 km

Left onto the old railway bed

The Poperinge to Hazebrouck line, now a car-free path. West to Sint-Jan-ter-Biezen.

3,5 km

Sint-Jan-ter-Biezen

Chapel hamlet. Leave the railway and head north: the lane climbs gently past the hop farms towards Blokstraat and Gravendreef.

6,1 km

Right into Gravendreef

The dreef runs straight at the wood. Limited parking at the end; from here it is walkers only.

6,5 km

Couthofbos

Sessile oaks, some of them very old; the signs say the oldest tree of Poperinge stands here. Take the path north-east through the wood.

Quiet wood. You may have it to yourself.

7,0 km

Out at the north side

Leave the wood towards Couthoflaan, cross it with care (it is the busiest road of the day) and follow the lane north towards Canadabos.

9,3 km

Canadabos

A wood with a strange history: under the leaves lies a concrete launch pad for V1 flying bombs from 1944. Information board at the entrance.

The northernmost point.

10,4 km

De Lovie

The castle of De Lovie, 19th century, with its park and ponds, today a care home. The lane skirts the park; the gardens are open on weekdays.

10,8 km

South on Provenseweg

From the castle gate head south-east, then south along Provenseweg, a long straight lane towards Poperinge.

12,0 km

Long straight south

Hop fields and farms. The town appears ahead; stay on the lane until the ring road.

12,9 km

Cross the ring road

A short stretch beside the ring, then Provenseweg continues south-west out of town. Then Provenstraat, Helleketelweg and the dreef home.

17,3 km

Home

Boots off. 17,3 km in all, 2,0 km of it unpaved.
